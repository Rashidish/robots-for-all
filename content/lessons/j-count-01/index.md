## Predict

Lay out five **forward** cards in a row on the floor. Then show the **×5** card next to just one forward card.

Ask the class:

- Do these two rows tell the robot to do the same thing?
- Which row is quicker to make?

## Run

1. Tape a row of six squares on the floor with the water in the last square.
2. A child is the rhino. The class shows the ×5 card and one forward card. The rhino takes five steps, counting out loud.
3. Did the rhino reach the water?
4. Open the mission **Count to the water** and tap the same two blocks: **×5**, then **Forward**. Press **Play**.

## Try

1. Play the mission with five forward blocks instead. You still reach the water, but with one star. Why not two?
2. Move the water closer on the floor. Which repeat card do you need now?
3. Try **×2** and **×3** with forward: how far does the rhino go?

## Make

In pairs, one child puts the water on any square of the floor row. The other counts the squares and makes the shortest plan with one repeat card. Walk it to check. Then swap.

Colour the stars and a face on the mission sheet.

## Teacher notes

- A **repeat** (a loop) is one of the biggest ideas in coding. Connect it to maths: "×5 forward" is like 5 groups of 1 step.
- The ×N card repeats only the card straight after it. If a child puts ×3 before a turn, the robot turns three times: let them find this by testing.
- Two stars means the fewest blocks. Children who reach the water with one star are not wrong; ask them to find a shorter way.
