## Predict

Everyone stands up and faces the board. Hold up the **turn right** card: everyone makes a quarter turn to the right, without stepping forward. Now **turn left**.

Ask the class:

- After a turn, is the robot in a new square, or the same square?
- Which way is the robot facing now?

## Run

1. Tape a three-by-three grid of squares on the floor. Put a rock (a crumpled paper ball) in the middle square and the school picture in a corner.
2. A child is the robot, standing in the opposite corner. The class holds up cards one at a time: forward, forward, turn right, forward, forward.
3. Did the robot reach the school? Turns don't move the robot to a new square, they only change the way it faces.

## Try

1. Open the mission **Round the corner** on the screen.
2. Plan the cards first, then tap the blocks into the row and press **Play**.
3. If the bus turns the wrong way, stand up and face the same way as the bus. Which arm points to the school?
4. Can you find a way with five blocks? That's the best way, with two stars.

## Make

In pairs, use the tape grid. One child puts the school picture in any square. The other plans the arrow cards with at least one turn, then walks the plan. Then swap.

Colour the stars and a face on the mission sheet.

## Teacher notes

- Left and right are hard at this age. A sticker or a wool band on the right wrist helps.
- Turns are a quarter turn, on the spot. Children often step forward as they turn: practise turning "like a robot" with both feet staying in the square.
- The robot on the screen turns the same way as a child facing the same way, not the way the screen looks. Facing the same way as the robot is the most useful trick to teach.
