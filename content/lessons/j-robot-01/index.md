## Predict

Make a row of four squares on the floor with masking tape, each about one big step long. Put the flower picture in the last square.

Choose one child to be the **robot**. The robot stands in the first square, facing the flower.

Hold up three **forward** cards. Ask the class:

- If the robot takes one step for each card, where will the robot stop?
- Will the robot reach the flower?

## Run

1. The robot takes one step forward for each card you point to. Point to the cards slowly, one at a time.
2. Did the robot stop on the flower? Clap if it did!
3. Now hold up a **back** card. The robot takes one step back. Where is the robot now?
4. Let a few children have a turn being the robot.

## Try

1. Open the mission **Three steps to the flower** on the phone, tablet or projector.
2. Tap **Forward** three times. Each tap puts a block in the row.
3. Press **Play** and watch the bee move one square for each block.
4. Tap **Back**, then **Play**. What happens? Tap a block in the row to take it out again.

## Make

In pairs, one child is the robot and one child is the **programmer**. The programmer puts arrow cards in a row on the floor, and the robot follows them to reach the flower. Then swap.

Colour a face on the mission sheet: how did it go?

## Teacher notes

- A **robot** only does what the instructions say, one step at a time. This is the big idea of the lesson: say it often.
- Grade R attention spans are short: keep each part to about five minutes, and move on while children still want more.
- Print the arrow cards and the mission sheet from the links on this page. The pictures have no words, so they work in every language.
- If you have only one phone, put it on a projector or let children come up one at a time while the others are robots on the floor.
